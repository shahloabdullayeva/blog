I love early mornings, smell of the air free from the disgusting people and the quiet they bring. "Disgusting" is not targeted to anybody, more of a general observation about humanity ad a group. There's a lot of reasoning to that but can't quite put it to words. I think I love mornings because I am seeking emptiness and cleanness that other time of the day I can't find. I come to this place once or twice a week or more. On the way I caught myself in a bittersweet moment when I saw a man with brown german shepherd probably a mix with golden retriever. I used to have a best friend Alfa and he's my uncles dog and it brought this unintentional nostalgia and times we shared,  went to mountains alone because I was safe. Misanthropy itself, I don't know where did that come from, it's not intentional and I wouldn't debate on this topic philosophically. Whole philosophy itself is meaningless. 

I watched A Separation ( جدایی نادر از سیمین, Judoi Nodir az Simin). How masterfully written and performed. It did deserve Oscar back in 2012 as I see. It's played so naturally almost feels like I am literally there, part of a cast. Did they improvise it? Someone commented in Vasilbros channel: why Iranian movies are so full of mysery, melancholy. I rather say it's filled with reality. Some call it artistic pessimism but I don't quite agree with that. But it doesn't mean life is misery and that's reality. There's an actual history behind it. Post 1980s in Iran has heavy censorship so they couldn't explicitly show political stuff and they found a way to show them in human struggles and emphasize in ordinary people. So that's actually how life is to ordinary people. These films doesn't sort it to happy or sad it just shows all. Iranian movies aren't only sadness, haven't you watched the part where Nadir played with his children? Also the fact that no one in it is wrong. Nadir is not cruel for staying because he simply cannot abandon a father who no longer knows his name. Simin is not selfish for wanting to leave because she is a mother trying to buy her daughter a future. Razieh is not a liar she's just a frightened devout woman trapped between her faith and her need.  Termeh a literal child who's trying her best to keep her family unseperated but it ends without telling us which parent she chooses, two of them waiting in the hallway.

Someday I want to live in that country myself.
