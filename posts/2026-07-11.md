# July 11, 2026

I wanted to go far, very far so I could give myself some life. That was the whole reason. People keep telling me that I have "dead-inside vibes" and if I want to kill myself and instead of brushing it off I tell them the truth, no I don't want to die, not yet, because if I think about it, I haven't lived yet. It gives me this small weird hope that I genuinely hate, because hope is embarrassing and it asks something of you, but it's there I will get rid of it soon.

So I went. My closest friend lives a thousand kilometres from me and I actually did it! I got myself there, which if you know me is not nothing. It was unbearably hot where she is, the kind of heat I have no tolerance for and I would prefer to die in Antarctica instead of this heat. It makes me slow and stupid. And I had a good time. I want to write that plainly because I don't say it often and I don't want to dress it up: I genuinely had a good time. There is something about dragging your body somewhere it does not want to go, sweating through a heat you can't stand, just to sit next to one person who actually knows you that is maybe the most alive I have felt in a while. And she gave me something before I left, a poem book of Forough Farrokhzad in uzbek and I still don't have the words for what that did to me and that is a strange thing to admit as someone whose whole job is finding words. I read it the entire way back. There is something in Forough that goes straight. I don't know how she knew that was the exact thing to put in my hands. No I actually know how she knows, it was attention. And as Simone Weil said attention, taken to its highest degree is the same thing as prayer. Maybe that is what closest means.

Also one thing I keep wanting to tell someone. I bought the ticket from avtoticket.uz. And while I was in there trying to download the ticket, I saw interesting stuff. And according to Claude, the whole thing was running in debug mode, in production and one wrong turn handed me a stack trace and sitting inside that stack trace was its entire configuration file. The database login, the app key, a token for their Telegram bot, the credentials for the service that sends the SMS codes to your phone. I actually have everything to just get my laptop and turn down everything but something really keeps me from doing that. And it might just be laziness (or fear of letting my mom down)

Anyways, it's true then, the thing everyone says and I never believed. You have to let things go. Physically and mentally I am trying. The physical part is almost the easier one, you buy the ticket, you get on you go. The mental part is the one I keep failing at, the letting go of people and of versions of things that already ended but at least I am facing the right direction now, which is more than I could say a month ago. Now I am making time for the real stuff again. I have a new translation coming out soon, Poe again, but a shorter one this time. (Stay tuned.) I won't say which. It feels good to have something that is just mine, that no grade and no manager gets to touch that I do only because I love the sentences and want them to exist in Uzbek. That, apparently, is what living looks like for me. And doing some technical translations as well.

I am writing this from Safia near IT Park, by the window, watching everyone else's life go past. There is an Uzum courier out there in the full purple outfit, purple bag and all and he looks so stressed, checking his phone every second waiting for an order or a client. The phone is plugged into a powerbank so he can keep going without stopping. It's 4:25 now so he isn't stopping any time soon, dinner rush is coming and the orders will only pile up. Good spot for him at least, right by IT Park where the offices are full of men far too busy thinking about work to think about anything else, men for whom food is a side project they remember only when their stomach makes them. A couple walks by, both look Russian, both blonde, shorts and water bottles, the guy in a white backpack covered in little gen-z symbols, and they look light in a way I notice. A man around forty with his daughter, just walking, going nowhere in particular. And a Coca-Cola truck parked outside Korzinka, waiting to unload. I have to laugh at that truck a little because last week I wrote a whole thing about not being able to escape this machine, Coca-Cola in every corner shop and at every wedding, the whole system we're already soaked in, and here it is, twenty metres from me while I sit and drink something and watch. This year I actually wanted to do my research on Coca-Cola specifically. There were reports that even consumed within normal limits their products had some concerning health issues, something to do with refrigeration, cold chain problems and I got curious because I have honestly never seen a reefer truck here maybe they exist, I read it online and couldn't be sure. I decided it wasn't solid enough to build a capstone on, so I left it and wrote about Agile teams instead and guess what I got a terrible grade for it. And now here's the joke: this summer I was shortlisted for an internship by Coca Cola İçecek and the thing that connects me to that exact truck, is supply chain. Which I do not want to do. But it is so much better than the customer service job I'm in right now that if they call me for an interview I'll probably say yes. That's the part I keep circling. I wrote an essay about not wanting to feed the machine and I might go work inside its logistics, and I'll do it because it's the better compromise.
