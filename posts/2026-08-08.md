# August 8, 2026

People calling me weird quite a lot recently, what's wrong with that? I went looking for something that would tell me my head is normal and I found Aristotle, who says it is not. I didn't read Metaphysics but from online resources I found, in the fourth book of that Metaphysics: the same thing cannot be and not be at the same time and in the same way, and he said it is the surest rule there is and said you cannot even argue against it without using it while you argue (Aristotle, 1924). I know how complicated it sounds, I thought about it for a whole day to finally get it. So either I love you or I do not, the door is open or it is closed.

In 1999 two psychologists, Kaiping Peng and Richard Nisbett, decided to check whether everybody actually thinks like this. They gave American and Chinese students pairs of statements that could not both be true and watched what happened. The Americans chose one of the two and then believed it more strongly than they had believed it before they were shown the pair at all. The Chinese students accepted both and carried on (Peng and Nisbett, 1999). Accepting. The researchers gave the second way of thinking a name, naive dialecticism, and it comes down to three ideas: that everything is always changing, that contradiction is the normal state of things and not a mistake in them, and that no part of anything can be understood apart from what it is attached to (Spencer-Rodgers et al., 2009). Under that rule, love turning into its opposite is not a betrayal of love. It is just what things do when you leave them out in the weather.

There is a stranger study still. In 2006 Christina Fong put people into a state of mixed feeling, good and bad at once, and then gave them a word puzzle where you are shown three unrelated words and have to find the one word that connects all three. The people who felt two things at once did better at it. The happy ones did not. The sad ones did not. Neither of those made any difference at all (Fong, 2006). Her explanation is that feeling two opposite things at the same time is such an odd inner weather that the mind takes it as a sign that the situation itself is odd and starts looking for connections it would not usually bother looking for. So a head full of contradictions is either a logical impossibility, or an ordinary way of thinking that most of the world manages fine, or a small advantage, depending on who is holding the clipboard. None of the three tell you what it is like from the inside. What it is like from the inside is this.

I read Russian literature almost only and I cannot read in Russian. Both of those are true and I have stopped explaining it to people. The language lives in my ear and dies on the page. Everything I love in those books arrived secondhand, through somebody else's translation, in somebody else's voice, and lately I have started wondering whether anything has ever reached me directly or whether all of it has come through a middleman? I go to sleep with a plan for the morning and I wake up and the plan belongs to somebody else, some hopeful girl who did not know how the body would feel at six. I know what I should do. I have known for months. There is no missing information. I could write the correct steps out for someone else in five minutes, in a nice font, and they would work for that person, and I sit here with the same five minutes and the same steps and do none of it.
