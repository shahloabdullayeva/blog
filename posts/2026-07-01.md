# July 1, 2026

Whatever happens in my life, writing something is always restorative and meaningful for me.

I am planning to get a T-shirt with "I might be a horrible person but at least I am not a man", and it's a sarcastic quote for men of a certain group. And not all men should be offended because when they see it, they know exactly what kind of men I am talking about here. And Alan was like, heh you're a feminist.

Of course I am. Just not the kind he was picturing.

Because there is a version of feminism people carry around in their heads now, and it is not mine. The one that supposedly means a woman should never cook, never want children, never want a home of her own. I do not believe any of that. I think everyone should be able to cook. Everyone should be able to feed themselves, or earn enough to put food on their own table. That is not a women's job or a men's job. That is just being a grown adult who can take care of themselves. So no, I am not fighting so that women can do less. I am fighting so that no one is handed a script at birth and told this is who you have to be. When I say I am a feminist, I mean something simple and hard to argue with. Women are full human beings, with the same rights as everyone else. That is the entire claim. It is a question of basic human rights, and once you look at it that way, most of the noise around the word falls apart. I am not asking for women to be lifted above anyone. I am asking for the floor everyone else is already standing on. A human being is not one thing. A woman is not only beauty, and she is not only emotion. She has a mind. She has talent. She has a soul. When the world looks at a woman and sees only a face, only a body, only something soft and pleasant to look at, it is not paying her a compliment. It is quietly erasing most of her. To be treated as decoration is to be told that your inside does not count, that the thinking part, the working part, the part with ideas, can be skipped over. The right to be seen as a whole person instead of a surface is a human right. It is not a favor someone hands you for being pretty enough or quiet enough.

A woman also has the right to choose her own life. If she wants a career then it is her choice. If she wants to stay home and raise her children and build a family, that is her choice too and it is not a smaller life. I will never look down on a woman who chose that. She chose it. That is the whole point.  The problem is when the choice is made for her. The problem is when a woman wants the job, wants the chance, wants the room to show what she can do, and is told no not because she could not do it but because she is a woman. Not the woman who stayed home but the woman who was sent home when she wanted to stay and fight. I know that refusal from the inside. I am young (but am i really) and I work a job with a small title so everyone in the room looks down on you. But the title is not the work. The work I actually do would, in a healthy place, belong to four or five different people. I really do feel undervalued. Billing, writing the requirements, owning the product decisions. Handling every problem the customers bring. Being the first person every issue with the app comes to and dealing with the pricing and the outside vendors like EFS and WEX. One person doing the work of a whole department and still being spoken to as if she were the smallest and least important person in the building. I work with developers who treat my requests like background noise just because I am a woman and my work title is Customer Service who can make me feel foolish for asking and will ignore the very thing I was put there to ask for. I have lived in that gap between what I am capable of and what people assume I am capable of. The part that took me a long time to understand is that the gap has nothing to do with my ability. That is what being unseen feels like in real life. 

Women are taught who to be. You have to be pretty but not too pretty to attract men, be likeable but not a pick me, be less, quiet etc. Men are taught who not to be like do not be soft, do not cry, do not be gentle, do not be feminine in any way,do not cook or clean if you have a wife to do it for you. Do not show anything tender, because tenderness in a man gets treated like a flaw almost like something to be ashamed of. And also hate women if they're too emotional. Men have beauty inside them too. Real gentleness, real feeling, a whole inner world they are taught to bury before anyone can see it. They get punished for the parts of themselves that look anything like a woman as if being like a woman were the worst thing a person could be accused of. That insult runs in both directions at once. It tells men to hide half of who they are.  This is why I do not think feminism is women against men. A man who is not allowed to feel and a woman who is not allowed to think are both being robbed of a full life. The right to be a whole person, the strength and the softness, the mind and the heart, belongs to every single one of us. That is a human right too. And it is exactly why the best men I can imagine are feminists. Not because it is fashionable and not because they are generously handing women a gift from above. They are feminists because they understand, plainly, that their wife, their daughter, their sister, their friend deserves the same value and the same room in the world that they themselves were given for free. And because they are not afraid of their own softness. A man who lets himself be gentle, who can cook and clean and care for the people around him without feeling like it makes him smaller, who wants the woman beside him to be fully and loudly herself, is not a weak man. He is a free one. The men who feel threatened by equality are the ones still trapped inside the rules. The men who welcome it have already walked out of the cage and found there was never anything to fear out here. So when I say I am fighting for human rights, this is what I mean. I am not asking for women to stand above anyone. I am asking for something so basic it should not still need a fight. That a person, any person, be allowed to be all of who they are. To choose their own life. To be seen as more than a surface. To be soft if they are soft and strong if they are strong, to cook because they want to or build a career because they want to, without ever being told that the wrong half of them is showing.

That is the world I want. 

And yes, Alan. That makes me a feminist.
