# July 14, 2026

My translation is out. [The Oval Portrait](/translations/oval-portret) by Poe. I wanted to say something about how it went, because I lied to myself about this one. It is genuinely short, only a few pages, and I thought I could finish it in one sitting by the morning. I was very, very wrong. Terribly wrong.

I learned today that short means every sentence is load-bearing and there is nowhere to hide. Poe builds these long, suspended sentences that seem to hold their breath for six lines before they land, and it was honestly hard to translate into Uzbek. Half the work was thinking. A single word could eat a whole evening. There were moments when I had to sit with a sentence for so long that it stopped feeling like language and started feeling like an argument with myself.

What do you call a château that is not quite a castle and not quite a palace? What do you do with “armorial trophies” when the whole heraldic world behind them does not exist in the language? The story itself is quiet horror, which is maybe the most difficult kind. The horror is not in the blood or the scream; it is in the stillness. A painter becomes so consumed with painting his young wife that he does not notice her fading in front of him. The more perfect the portrait becomes, the more life drains out of her, until the picture is finished and somehow alive, and art has fed on the living thing it was only trying to hold on to. He simply looks at the canvas instead of her for too long, and by the time he turns around it is already done.

I know this is a small audience, but if anyone actually read the translation, please send me a message about your thoughts on it, or if there is anything overall, through the [contact](contact.html) page. I would really like to know what landed and what did not.
