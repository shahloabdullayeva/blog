# September 30, 2026

I have been gone for a while and I would love to tell you there was a reason, a trip or a breakdown or a book deal, but there was not. I just did not write. The days went to work and sleep and the space between them and every night I told myself tomorrow and tomorrow is a very patient liar. Anyway I have a vacation now, one whole week and my plans for it are embarrassingly small. I want to sleep until my body stops asking for it, which may take the entire week and I want to go back to my translations, which have been sitting there looking at me the way unfinished things do and a few other things I keep promising myself. A week is not much. But it is the first time in a long time that the days are mine and not borrowed from somebody else's schedule and I am going to try very hard not to spend it on anything that watches me.

Now the thing I actually have to get out of my system. I hate Korzinka. I cannot boycott them because I use them far too much and that is the humiliating part, that I will be back inside one this week probably, but I want it written down somewhere that I hate them. I was at the self checkout and I was tired, the kind of tired where your hands are doing things and your head is somewhere else entirely and I did not scan one of the products. I did not notice. The door rang when I walked out and suddenly I was a shoplifter. I explained. I told them I did not know, that I was tired and probably was not paying attention, that I had the money right there and would pay for it that second and they kept saying that it did not look like that. It did not look like that. I do not know what a tired person is supposed to look like to them. They wanted to file it with the police, for something I did not even do on purpose and I stood there being talked about like a case. And I will be fair because I am always fair even when I do not want to be. If that door had not rung they would never have known and if everybody did what I did by accident the company would be in trouble, I understand that, I did not fail statistics. But they could have just let me pay and leave. That was the whole solution and it was sitting right there. Instead they took a picture of me and put it up on their board, which looked exactly like the wall of shame from the movies, rows of faces of people who are supposed to be criminals and now mine is one of them. The man said it would not be shared. I know for a fact that it will be. I have never been so embarrassed in my life and I do not want to walk into that Korzinka again or any Korzinka and the only reason I will is that Havas and Makro do not work 24/7 and I work until midnight. And here is the part that makes it almost funny. The guy who checked my bag did not put back one of the things I actually paid for. I noticed and I did not go back for it, because I was not going to stand in that place one minute longer than I had to. So technically they owe me. Technically I am the one who was robbed that night, I was just too ashamed to ask for it back. File that with the police.

Something better. They are showing the Linkin Park film at Cinematica. It is called Unshatter and it opened in cinemas around the world today, directed by Joe Hahn from the band himself and it is about the comeback after Chester, the recording of From Zero and Emily Armstrong and Colin Brittain joining, with half of it being the concert they played in São Paulo last November. It is a limited run so I am not taking chances. I hope I can go on Saturday. I am not sure how I will feel watching it. Linkin Park is not a band for me, it is a period of my life and I do not know yet whether I want to see what happens to something like that after the person at the centre of it is gone and the rest of them decide to keep going anyway. Maybe that is exactly why I should go.

Also we are going to play volleyball today and you can all come. I do not know why I am announcing this on a blog almost nobody reads, but on the chance that one of the few of you is in Tashkent and bored, it is near Beruniy metro, on the territory of Milliy University (the National University) and it is open to anyone. I cannot promise that I am any good. I can promise that I will be there.

And I finished Dazai. I wrote last time that I had just started The Setting Sun and that I was seeing all of it as anime and I kept seeing it that way until the very end, which is a strange way to watch something this sad. Kazuko is losing interest in life. What stayed with me is not what happens to her but how quiet all of it is. Nobody in that book screams. They are losing everything they were and they lower their voices about it and I kept thinking that this is what grief actually looks like most of the time, not a scene but a person setting the table for someone who is not coming back. There is a kind of tiredness in it that I know too well, the feeling of being born into a world that has already decided it does not need you and then the smaller cruelty of still having to get up in it every morning. Somewhere in the middle of all that she chooses to live anyway, not because anything got better but almost out of stubbornness and that is the part I think the book is really about. Hope that does not wait to be earned. Dazai killed himself a year after he wrote her and I do not know what to do with that except notice that he gave her the one thing he could not keep for himself. I closed it heavier than when I opened it and somehow less alone, which is the most I ever ask of a book.
