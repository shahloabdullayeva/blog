# June 17, 2026

I like to categorize my thoughts. It gets messy anyway and they follow me everywhere, even if i'm on a call with a client saying "thank you for holding, let me pull up your account" or  in a crowded bus in the scorching hot, even when i'm trying to sleep. the whole time there's a second conversation going that never turns off when you hang up the call or step off the bus or finally get home and lie down, it just keeps running in the background like a tab you forgot to close.

I used to think sleep was one way to get rid of them, but it's only halfway since the brain keeps thinking even in our sleep, the mind never actually rests and it just takes whatever happened during the day and stitches it into a dream and we end up thinking the exact same thing the entire night. I looked some of this up because i wanted to know if i was the only one, but it turned out it isn't just me and there's a whole theory about how if you tell yourself not to think about something, let's say a white bear, then the white bear becomes the only thing the brain will keep thinking about.

even when you finally stop and rest your brain refuses to go silent, because there's this whole network inside that lights up the second you do nothing and it starts replaying conversations you've already had a hundred times and rehearsing ones that are never going to happen, so being idle was never the same thing as being off, it's just when the loudest thoughts finally get the room to themselves, and on top of that a song gets stuck and loops for days and old memories show up uninvited because of a smell or a color, and then at night your brain takes the entire day and files it and sorts it and plays it back, which is part of why the things you think about the most are the ones that always find their way into your dreams, the mind just doing its filing and never once asking whether you actually wanted that folder opened again.

so since they don't leave, i decided i would at least sort them myself and give them names and i started seeing them in colors.

black thoughts are the heavy ones, the fears and the what ifs and the cruel little words my own head says to me that i would never in my life say out loud to another person. white thoughts are the good ones, the fantasies (though some of those are dark too), the memories, the plans that are never going to happen but still excite me, and the clean quiet ones, except those are always the first to leave. colorful thoughts are the internet sourced cringes, a meme, some stupid clip, a cursed phrase, all that time i spent scrolling past reels and shorts, though i did manage to cut my screen time down to 3 hours, and those cringe thoughts still live in my head and decide to resurface for absolutely no reason. and the pink ones are the worst ones, the ones i don't even want to write about.

all my mornings are mondays stuck in an endless february, but that one isn't a color and it isn't really a thought i can sort, it's just where i am right now.

And the person who can put a february like this into words better than anyone alive is Taylor, which is probably why i keep coming back to her, because she just got inducted into the songwriters hall of fame on june 11th and she's the youngest woman ever to make it in and the second youngest person in the whole history of it, right behind stevie wonder and it happened exactly twenty years after she put out tim mcgraw as a sixteen year old, which still gets to me, because people love to act like it all just fell into her lap when really she signed her first publishing deal at fourteen and has spent her entire life writing, song after song after song until something like sixty nine of them had landed in the top ten and when she got up there she said the writing was instinctual, that no one ever taught her how to do it and that in an industry obsessed with metrics and data and trying to predict what will trend, writers still have to trust their own human intuition, and i don't think i've needed to hear anything more than i needed to hear that.

what she does that genuinely wrecks me is that she takes the smallest, most specific, most embarrassing feeling, the kind you assume belongs only to you because it's too quiet or too humiliating to ever say out loud and she writes it down so precisely that millions of people read it and swear she's been reading their diary and that is the whole miracle of it, she turns the lonely thing into a shared thing, she takes your pink thoughts and your black ones and hands them back to you as a song so that they stop feeling like proof that something is wrong with you and just start feeling human and that is what she has done for me and for every single fan who ever felt too much and had nowhere to put it.

And fortnight is my current favorite right now.
