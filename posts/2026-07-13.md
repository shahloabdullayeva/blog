# July 13, 2026

I came home. It is cooler here and that is the first thing the village gives you before anything else. The evenings stretch out long and blue and you can breathe again. My grandparents are here. My uncle's wife and her children the little ones underfoot. My uncle himself is in Russia, working like half the men from here are always somewhere else, working. And I am happy to see them but then you sit down for tea and it starts. It always comes in sideways, over tea kindly, which is exactly what makes it so hard to push against. Someone has a brother, someone knows a good family. My uncle's wife wants me to marry her brother and I keep saying no and I feel bad every single time I say it and I have to keep reminding myself that I do not need a better reason than the one I have. I don't want to. That is completely a valid reason. It does not need a story attached to it, it does not need him to have done something wrong, it does not need justifying to anyone. I don't want to, and that should settle it! It's an emotional torture. What they are looking for is not a wife. What they are looking for is labour. They will tell you they want a girl with a diploma (that part is new) that times have changed and now see, girls should study. But the diploma is fucking decoration, it is proof of value, something to raise the price. Many men say they succeeded in life even without one, but there's really no way for a girl to try that. I myself has a terrible GPA, even tho our university teaches well, I had to survive and sometimes it costed me my time. For a girl, diploma is not a thing she is ever actually meant to use. What she is meant to do is cook and look after the house the cow and the sheep, and raise the children more or less alone because her husband is far too busy. Then also she has to work, so that she never has to ask her husband for money. Because if she asks, questions start. Why is she home? What does she even do all day? Better she earns her own little bit. They want an unpaid servant who happens to come with a certificate. When the children are grown she might be allowed to work in the fields on someone's land, the harder jobs, the ones no one bothers to call a career. Then when the children start to go to school she will be told to stay home. And then when money gets tight again because it always does, because children cost more the older they get, she will be sent back out to the same hard work. In and out, in and out according to what the household needs that year never once according to what she wants, because what she wants was never entered into the arithmetic in the first place.

I keep thinking about one of my aunts. She is applying to medical college now. At 31. She wanted this when she was young, at exactly the age you are supposed to want it and she was refused. Not for girls, you can't be working and all the usual sentences. And she went and lived an entire life inside those sentences and now she is quietly trying again and I am so glad for her I could cry. And I am also grieving for the ten years that were taken from her. Because if someone had simply let her go at eighteen, she could already be a doctor, or someone. She could already be the person she spent her whole life trying to become. She lost a decade that was never really about her at all and I am supposed to feel only the happiness now and not the loss but I feel both. And she is not the only one. You do not have to look far here for the other version, the one that does not end in a second chance the woman married off to a man she did not choose, worn down to almost nothing, divorced and then married again and treated badly the second time too, thin and always working, two children and no road back to parents who are old now and cannot hold her up, no way to stand on her own because standing on her own was never a thing she was allowed to build. She wanted to study once. She never got the chance. Girls don't study. And so it goes, one life after another after another, the same shape pressed onto each of them.

I don't think it is really about the diploma or the job in the end, I think it is about the fact that a worldview only widens when you are allowed to widen it. It changes when you learn things, when you meet people who are not exactly like you, when you are permitted to go somewhere and see something and come home a little different. It does not always happen through school sometimes a person changes because of who they happen to meet and I don't want to be simple about it. But learning is the surest way and learning is precisely the thing being kept from these women over and over and I have stopped believing that is an accident. Keep a person from learning and you keep their world the size of one courtyard. Maybe that is the whole point of it. So I keep saying no. And I feel bad and I say it anyway, because I have watched with my own eyes what a yes costs here and I am not going to walk into it wide awake just to spare a few people one uncomfortable afternoon. My mother understands at least partway. She does not force me.

Yeah it is cooler here...
