So this is the second attempt and I am telling you first that this one is the good one or at least it is about a good thing and if the sentences come out weird it is because I have not used this hand in a while. The eight hours obviously didn't work and I am still exhausted that part did not get fixed at all. Same shit to deal with everyday just four hours shorter and I still come home and lie down like something that has been carried in. So whatever this is, it is not the schedule. Now the part I actually sat down to write, which is that I did not write anything for a week and I had a theory about why and the theory turned out to be garbage. It's the oldest one in the book, that writers use their pain, childhood traumas. You write out of the wound, the writing is the wound talking and if the wound closes then the writing stops and the girl gets to be well or she gets to be a writer and not both. I have believed that for years without once checking it and this week I thought, there it is it really has arrived, I feel better and now I have nothing to write about that's disturbing my life. There's some as always but I learnt to deal with them. I sat down tonight and all of it is still here, every sentence I have ever been able to make is exactly where I left it and the week was empty because I was tired in the specific stupid way a job makes you tired and not because anything had been taken from me. I changed a little bit. I catch myself starting an optimistic thought which is really not my vibe at all. I do not like it. I have no idea who taught me that a person's usefulness runs on her damage but whoever it was did a good job.

And I want to be careful with the word happiness because it is a bad word and it does no work. According to a little research I did, some uncle named Tim Lomas went through other languages looking for what they use for feeling well and collected two hundred and sixteen words that will not come across into English (Lomas, 2016). Saudade in Portuguese, a longing for something absent with the pleasure still attached to the longing. Sisu in Finnish, what is left of a person after everything reasonable has run out. Mono no aware in Japanese, the ache that arrives when you notice a thing is going to end while it is still going on. Look at what those have in common. A good number of the entries carry sadness inside them as an ingredient and not as the opposite and the languages that built them were plainly not asking anybody to pick a side. English asks are you happy, yes or no, like the doctor asks you at a clinic. The word is so wide it cannot be wrong and so it tells you nothing, and when two people agree that they are happy they are very often describing two unrelated events.

I still have the Schopenhauer out from last time, which is my own fault, and he has a rule about all of this that I have carried around for years. He says satisfaction is only ever negative. You are never handed pleasure, what happens is that a want stops for a moment, and the second it stops there is nothing left to push against so it turns into boredom and a life swings back and forth between the two like a pendulum (Schopenhauer, 1969). For a long time that read to me as the last honest thing anybody wrote on the subject. This is the first time I have caught him being wrong about me. What happened to me was not a subtraction. Nothing was removed. Everything that was here in August is still precisely here, the flatness and the distance and everything.

Which brings me to the half I would rather people took from this post than the good news. The sadness did not leave, it moved over and made room. It is still underneath. I think it is in everybody and the reason I distrust the word happy is that people use it as though it meant the sound had stopped. It does not stop. What can happen is that something gets loud enough to sit on top of it for a while, and while that lasts the low sound is not gone, it is only not the only thing in the room anymore. And it will not last. Everything I have ever felt arrived and then went and this will do the same, and knowing it in advance has somehow not stopped me enjoying it which is new for me and humiliating.

In July I wrote that hope is embarrassing and that it asks something of you and that I would be getting rid of mine soon, and I did get rid of it, on schedule, and in June I wrote that a person should lose all hope and be free and I meant every word of it and I will tell you honestly that it was easier. It is much easier down there. Nothing can be taken from somebody who already put everything down. And I have found hope again and I am afraid of it. There is an entire literature on being afraid of it, which I found this week and read like a diagnosis. It is called aversion to happiness and the reasons people give for it are that being happy invites something bad to come and level it that it makes a person shallower or worse or less serious and that saying it out loud is asking for it (Joshanloo and Weijers, 2014). There is a scale for measuring it and one of the items says, more or less, that the person would rather not be too joyful because joy is usually followed by sadness. I read that item and did not think how sad. I thought, well, yes. Last time hope came for me it ended badly and I am not built to survive that twice.

I have two explanations for that and no idea which one is doing the work. The first is that I have BPD and the model behind the standard treatment for it is not exotic at all. You start out more sensitive than other people, you react harder, and you take much longer than everybody else to come back down to normal. Three sentences and they cover most of my life. The second is that I am an INFP, which I am aware is a horoscope with better marketing, and I am keeping it anyway. Either way the complaint comes out the same. Nobody takes it seriously. If it hurts, it hurts at twice the rate it hurts you, and when you say that out loud you get told you are too emotional, or too much, or my favourite, pathetic. As if it were something I picked.

I show almost nothing to almost anybody. The whole thing goes to a few people and it was never a burden I dropped on them it is the only thing I own that is worth handing over, and if you have ever been shown it then you were not being drained, you were being trusted. Which is the other thing that has not changed. I am still very far from nearly everybody and none of that improved. The list of people who actually reach me is short enough to count on one hand and I keep everybody else at the spacing I wrote about last time and I have made my peace with being the one who is difficult to have. But the count went up by one this year, and one is an enormous number when you start from where I start and a very small amount of light gets a very long way into a room like mine.

And then, because I would always rather have a finding than a feeling, I went looking for scientific permission to be afraid of my own good mood, and there is some. In 1979 Alloy and Abramson sat students in front of a button and a light and afterwards asked them how much control they had had over whether the light came on, and the depressed students were close to right and the ones who were not depressed overestimated themselves whenever the light had been coming on nicely, which is where the phrase sadder but wiser comes from (Alloy and Abramson, 1979). I was ready to frame it. It says the lens is not broken, it is calibrated. It says the price of cheering up is accuracy and accuracy is the only thing I have ever believed I was carrying. Anyways.

I read back through my own archive this week, which is a bad habit and I recommend it to no one. I really want to edit them but remember some things are left as is.
